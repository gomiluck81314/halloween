THE QUESTION

WHAT IS HALLOWEEN?

Halloween is celebrated mainly on October 31. Today, many people connect it with costumes, candy, pumpkins, parties, decorations, and spooky stories.

But the history of Halloween is much older, and much more complicated, than a night of candy. It grew over many centuries from different traditions in different places.

ANCIENT ROOTS

WHERE DID HALLOWEEN BEGIN?

Halloween has roots in the ancient Celtic festival of Samhain (said roughly like "SOW-in"). Samhain was linked to the end of the harvest and the move toward winter. It was especially important in Celtic-speaking regions such as Ireland and Scotland.

People believed this time of year had a special connection with supernatural forces. Move the slider to travel from harvest, through Samhain, into winter.

Samhain: What We Know, What People Believed, What Is Debated

📜 What the sources say

  • Samhain appears in early Irish literature written in the medieval period.
  • It is described as a major gathering and feast at the start of the dark half of the year.
  • Similar autumn festivals are recorded in Scotland, the Isle of Man, and Wales.

🔥 What people believed

  • According to folklore, the time between harvest and winter was a time of change.
  • People believed that spirits and beings from the "Otherworld" could be closer than usual.
  • Bonfires and feasts were part of later customs in many places.

❓ What is debated

  • Many written sources were recorded centuries later, often by Christian writers.
  • Historians still discuss how much of the later folklore matches ancient practice.
  • This is why we say Halloween has roots in Samhain, not that it simply is Samhain.

DISGUISES

WHY DO WE WEAR COSTUMES?

Some folklorists connect Halloween costumes with older traditions of disguising yourself. In some places, people believed that wearing a disguise could help them avoid, or blend in with, spirits that might be wandering. Later, in Ireland and Scotland, people also went from house to house in disguise, a custom often called guising. Historians still discuss how these customs are connected.

Today, costumes are mostly about entertainment, creativity, and self-expression. Hover over a character to see how it came to Halloween.

JACK-O'-LANTERNS

FROM TURNIPS TO PUMPKINS

Jack-o'-lantern traditions are connected to older European customs of carving vegetables. Originally, people in Ireland and Scotland carved turnips and other root vegetables. After the tradition became popular in America, pumpkins became common because they were larger and easier to carve.

WHY DO WE SAY "TRICK OR TREAT"?

Modern trick-or-treating developed from older European traditions in which people visited homes and received food or treats. The modern form became especially popular in North America during the 20th century.

FAITH & FOLK CUSTOM

HALLOWEEN & CHRISTIANITY

The word "Halloween" comes from All Hallows' Eve. "Hallows" is an old word for saints. Halloween is not simply "from Christianity" or "from the Celts." Its history is a combination of older European customs and Christian traditions that mixed over many centuries.

OCT 31

All Hallows' Eve

The evening before All Saints' Day. In Christian tradition, the eve of a feast was a time of preparation and prayer.

NOV 1

All Saints' Day

A feast to honor all the saints. Church tradition moved it to November 1 in the 8th and 9th centuries, though historians discuss the exact details.

NOV 2

All Souls' Day

A day to remember and pray for the dead. It became widely observed from about the year 1000.

Careful history. Scholars still debate how directly Samhain and the Christian feasts are linked. Some see a strong link, others see a gradual mixing of customs. The honest answer: Halloween is a blend, shaped by many cultures and beliefs.
ACROSS THE ATLANTIC

FROM EUROPE TO AMERICA

Immigration helped carry Halloween-related customs to North America. In the 1800s, especially after the Great Famine in Ireland began in 1845, large numbers of Irish people moved to the United States and Canada, along with many Scots. They brought songs, games, stories, and autumn customs with them. In the new land, these traditions mixed with other cultures and slowly changed.

1. Irish & Scottish customsBonfires, games, guising, divination games, and folk stories were part of autumn gatherings.
2. New traditions in AmericaIn some early colonies, Halloween was not widely celebrated. It spread more in the 1800s, mixing harvest festivals, ghost stories, and community parties.
3. Modern HalloweenAmerican popular culture shaped Halloween into a mostly fun, community-based holiday and spread it around the world.

OUR OWN HALLOWEEN

HALLOWEEN IN JAPAN

Halloween in Japan developed differently from its European origins. It is often more about costumes and entertainment than about the original seasonal or religious meaning. This is one example of cultural adaptation: a tradition travels, and each society shapes it in its own way.

🎭

COSTUME EVENTS

Costume parades and parties are central. The Kawasaki Halloween Parade, started in the 1990s, is one well-known example.

🎢

THEME PARKS

Large theme parks run special seasonal shows, decorations, and limited foods every autumn.

🛍️

SHOPPING & SEASONAL GOODS

Shops, cafés, and stores sell Halloween-themed sweets, decorations, and costumes for weeks before October 31.

📱

SOCIAL MEDIA

Photos of costumes and decorations spread quickly online, making creativity and sharing a big part of the fun.

🏙️

CITY GATHERINGS

Popular areas such as Shibuya draw big crowds. In recent years, local governments have asked visitors to follow rules for safety and consideration.

🏮

A CULTURAL ECHO

Japan has its own tradition of remembering ancestors: Obon, in summer. It is a different festival, but it shows that many cultures connect seasons with memory.
